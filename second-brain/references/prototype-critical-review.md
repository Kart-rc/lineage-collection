# Throughline Prototype: Critical Review

A self-inflicted adversarial review (v0.9, "pre-leadership") of the Throughline proposal, read against
the prototype, roadmap, and PRDs before they go to senior leadership. The review judges the pitch on
three axes — ease of use for the actual audience, scalability at enterprise size, and the credibility
of the 24-month plan — with the explicit goal of surfacing what a skeptical CFO, CTO, or VP will
challenge and fixing it first.

The bottom line it lands on: the concept and prototype are strong and well ahead of a typical pitch,
but three things will draw fire in the room —

1. **A build-vs-buy question that isn't pre-empted** — the pitch never argues why an off-the-shelf
   catalog or lineage product doesn't suffice.
2. **An estimate-vs-staffing mismatch** — the level-of-effort numbers and the proposed team capacity
   don't reconcile, which reads as inconsistency.
3. **A 24-month time-to-value with no early business win** — nothing in the plan gives leadership a
   reason to keep funding at quarter one.

None are judged fatal; all are fixable before the meeting. Findings are graded by severity and each
pairs "the challenge" with a "remedial action." Section 05 turns them into a remediation checklist
ordered by what protects the funding decision — the first five items are must-fix, the rest strengthen
the case. Section 06 deliberately names the assets ("what's already strong — lead with these") as
genuine differentiators to open the pitch with.

The document's role in the suite is structural: it is the input that the Remediation Plan converts
into owned, dated actions, and its critiques (coverage, identity resolution, capacity) are cited by
the engineering deep dive as internal sources.
